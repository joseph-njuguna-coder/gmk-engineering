Mill installation

Five checks before commissioning a new maize milling line

Commissioning is where a new line proves it can deliver safe, stable throughput—not simply switch on. Use these checks to prepare your team, equipment, and site for handover.

Eng. David Mwangi

Project Engineer, GMK Engineering

Published

12 June 2026 · 7 min read

A commissioning plan protects your investment by turning design intent into verified operating performance. Before product enters the system, make sure every critical decision has an owner and a measurable acceptance point.

1. Confirm the production target and product flow

Start with the promised throughput in tonnes per day, then trace material flow from intake through cleaning, milling, sifting, packing, and dispatch. Confirm that transfer points, surge bins, and bagging capacity support the same production target. A high-capacity roller mill will not compensate for a bottleneck at packaging or dispatch.

Site note

Define a realistic acceptance run: product type, moisture range, hours of continuous operation, target yield, and allowable stoppages.

2. Verify power quality, protection, and control logic

Electrical commissioning should cover load calculations, panel protection settings, cable termination, earthing continuity, phase rotation, and emergency-stop logic. The operator should be able to understand what every alarm means and how to return the process to a safe operating state.

3. Test dust extraction before full production

Dust control is a production, safety, and maintenance issue. Inspect ducting joints, fan rotation, filter condition, collection points, and access doors. Test the system under normal material loading rather than relying on an empty-line demonstration.

4. Train operators at the equipment

Training should cover routine inspection, lubrication, start-up and shutdown sequences, common alarms, housekeeping, and escalation routes. Pair every task with a clear checklist so the shift team does not depend on memory when production pressure rises.

5. Close handover with evidence, not assumptions

Ask for test records, operating manuals, electrical drawings, spare-parts schedules, and a maintenance plan tailored to the installed equipment. Agree the defect-resolution process before practical completion, including who responds and how quickly.

GMK Engineering supports Kenyan mill owners from site assessment through commissioning and aftercare. A structured handover lets your plant team start confidently and sustain performance long after the installation crew leaves site.
